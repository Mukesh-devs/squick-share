FeaturesThe real app, with sample devices

Sharing that feels built into your Mac.

Receive

Check the PIN, then accept.

When a phone shares with your Mac, SQuick Share shows who is sending, what and how big, with a 4-digit PIN. If it matches the phone, accept from the window, the notification or the menu bar. Files land in Downloads and are never opened for you.

  • Accept or decline right from the notification
  • Always accept from your own phone, if you choose
  • A file with the same name is never overwritten

4 digits on both screens, so you know it's them

Send

Drop files right on a phone.

Phones, tablets and PCs nearby show up as tiles. Drop files, folders or text on one, click it to choose files, or right-click in Finder and pick Share › SQuick Share. Each transfer shows its speed and the time left, with Cancel one click away.

  • Whole folders, with their structure kept
  • Links and text as well as files
  • Phones appear sooner: Bluetooth listens for them

30 MB/s from Mac to phone on 5 GHz Wi-Fi

History

Everything you shared, by day.

History keeps every file, link and text you sent or received, grouped by day, with the device it came from. Double-click to open, drag a file out to Finder, or search and filter to what you received or sent.

  • Received links open only when you click them
  • Show any file in Finder
  • Transfers that didn't go through say why

1 click from a transfer to its file in Finder

DevelopersOpen source, written from scratch in Swift

Read it, build it, script it.

SQuick Share speaks Google's Quick Share protocol through its own Swift package: no UI, 81 tests, and every protocol constant traced to Google's open-source code. The same package has a command-line tool that sends, receives and finds devices from Terminal.

receiveReceive in Terminal, into a folder you choose
sendSend files or text to a nearby device by name
browseList the Quick Share devices around you

Terminal — QuickShareCore 
# Receive into Downloads, with the details
$ swift run squick-share-cli receive --verbose

# Send a video to a phone nearby
$ swift run squick-share-cli send --to "Pixel 9" holiday.mov

# Build the app and install it
$ ./scripts/build-release.sh --install

PrivacyLocal and encrypted

0 servers in between.

Your files go straight from one device to the other.

No accountOpen the app and share. Nothing to sign up for, on either device.
No trackingNo analytics, no telemetry, no cloud. It only talks to devices on your network.
Encrypted end to endFresh keys for every transfer, and a PIN to confirm the other device.

After a P-256 key exchange, every message is AES-256 encrypted and HMAC-SHA256 signed. Incoming names are cleaned up, files are written only inside the folder you choose, and the app runs in the macOS App Sandbox with the Hardened Runtime. Open source under the MIT License.

Install in two minutes.

  1. 1 Copy it to Applications Open the .dmg and drag SQuick Share onto the Applications folder.
  2. 2 Allow it to open, once It's free and not notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm it: open it, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. Or run: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/SQuick Share.app"
  3. 3 Say yes to the local network When macOS asks to find devices on your local network, click Allow. Allow notifications and Bluetooth too, so phones find your Mac sooner.
  4. On your Android phone: nothing Quick Share is already there. Share anything, tap Quick Share and pick your Mac.

QuestionsShort answers

Good to know.

Something else? Ask on GitHub

Is it really free?

Yes. SQuick Share is open source under the MIT License, free for personal and commercial use, with no paid tier and no account.

Do I need an app on my phone?

No. It works with Quick Share, which is already on Android phones: share anything, tap Quick Share and pick your Mac. It's tested with Google's Quick Share in both directions; Pixel and Samsung phones use the same protocol.

My phone doesn't see my Mac. What now?

Check that both are on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network) and that you clicked Allow when macOS asked about the local network. Then choose Visibility › Announce Again from the menu bar icon. On Xiaomi and Redmi phones, use Google's Quick Share, not Xiaomi Share. More help

Why does macOS warn me the first time?

SQuick Share is signed ad hoc rather than notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm it once: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. The disk images are built by GitHub Actions from the public source.

Does it work with iPhone, Windows, or over the internet?

It's for Macs and Android devices on the same Wi-Fi. Transfers over the internet, Wi-Fi Direct and Bluetooth aren't supported. Quick Share for Windows uses the same protocol and should work, but hasn't been tested yet.
